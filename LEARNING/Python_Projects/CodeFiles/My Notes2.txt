1. Before Applying ARIMA Model, Time Series Data must be Stationary.  i.e Its Statistical Properties like mean and Variance should remain same across the period.
2. p,d and q used as parameters in ARIMA take non negative Integer values.
3. we are able to fit the model to any time series based on this combination of values.

4. AR: The time series is linearly regressed on its own past values
	p implies the number of past values included in the AR Model

   I : If not stationary, the time series can be differenced to become stationary, i.e compute the differences between consecutive observations
	d implies the number of times the time series is differenced

   MA: The time series is regressed on the past forecast errors
	q implies the number of past forecast errors included in the MA Model

5. ACF  : AutoCorrelation Function , which is the correlation of the time series with its lags(i.e past values)
   PACF : Partial Auto Correlation Function, which is the partial correlation of the time series with its lags, after removing the effects of lower order           lags between them.

6. After analysing the Original and Smoothed Time series data through ACF,PACF and ADF Tests, if pValue <=0.05, it is aasumed to be stationary.
if not, try with the differencing order =2, but not go beyond that, will be ineffective.

7. If the PACF plot has a significant spike at lag p, but not beyond; the ACF Plot decays more gradually. This may suggest an ARIMA(p,d,0) model
if the ACF plot has a significant spike at lag q, but not beyond, the PACF Plot decays more gradually. This may suggest an ARIMA(0,d,q) model
