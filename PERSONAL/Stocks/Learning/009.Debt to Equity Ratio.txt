Definition: The debt-equity ratio is a measure of the relative contribution of the creditors and shareholders or 
owners in the capital employed in business. Simply stated, ratio of the total long term debt 
and equity capital in the business is called the debt-equity ratio.

It can be calculated using a simple formula:

Debt Equity Ratio = Total Liabilities / Shareholders's Equity

Description: This financial tool gives an idea of how much borrowed capital (debt) 
can be fulfilled in the event of liquidation using shareholder contributions. 

It is used for the assessment of financial leverage and soundness of a firm 
and is typically calculated using previous fiscal year's data.

A low debt-equity ratio is favorable from investment viewpoint as 
it is less risky in times of increasing interest rates. 
It therefore attracts additional capital for further investment and expansion of the business.
